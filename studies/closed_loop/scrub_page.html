Odin Planner Replay

Six stress worlds, driven closed-loop: ostrich physics as the robot, the measured Odin dToF ray table as the sensor, a probabilistic belief map as the only thing the planner sees. Every frame is what the robot actually believed and decided, every frame recorded. The robot's own controller, read from its params file, with the robust margin split by cause: walls eroded hard, tilt and belly clearance charged. The six stress worlds: 6/6 reach, none touching a wall. The two trap worlds (corridor, false door) put a dead end 18 m out behind the opening aimed at the goal. The 2.8 m corridor could not be turned in forward-only: a spin is not in place for this robot (two drive wheels, a dragged rear wheel -- 0.44–0.9 m sideways per 120–150°), so the stuck turning run met the wall at ~120°; at 4.0 m the current stack turns and leaves 3/3, 626–701 frames, no contact. False door, window-bound: 0/4, once the dead end scrolls out of the 20 m window it is forgotten and the robot drives back in. With the coarse map anchored to the world (walls sealed from either side, a remembered wall never overwritten by a poorer view, the ring kept while the goal is walled off): 2/3 reach, in and back out through the false door, round by the real one, no wall contact; the third is trapped forward-only inside its own turning clearance after a late correction. With the turn-first brake (forward speed cut while the route lies more than 45° off the heading, so a big turn is a spin, not an arc that advances): 3/3, turning back at x = 9–13 the moment the map seals the back wall. With the shadow bridge (an unseen run of at most two blocks between two sealed wall blocks is the wall, so a wall seen ending in shadow no longer reads as a door) and the sim's uncertainty terms at the robot's values: one stop, one turn, out, 514–538 frames. The robot turns red while it touches a wall.

The view is a chase camera on the belief window — — across, robot-centred, recentred in whole cells as it drives. Ground the robot mapped earlier scrolls out of it, which is why the driven path runs off into empty space. The routing and blocked layers cover only the inner —, because a fine window larger than the sensor's reliable coverage plans over whatever filled the unobserved cells rather than over terrain. Each layer is scaled against its own range in that frame, printed under the map: a colour means a slightly different value from one frame to the next, which is what lets centimetres of relief stay visible before the robot has found anything taller.
